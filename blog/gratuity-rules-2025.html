Gratuity Rules for FY 2026-27: Formula, ₹25 Lakh Tax-Free Limit and the New Labour Codes

Gratuity is a lump sum your employer pays as a reward for long, continuous service — and for many salaried Indians it is one of the largest single payouts of their career. The rules changed significantly in 2025: the tax-free limit for private sector employees went up, and the four new Labour Codes that took effect on 21 November 2025 widened the definition of "wage" and made fixed-term employees eligible after just one year. This guide explains who qualifies, how to calculate your gratuity step by step, how it is taxed, and what the Labour Codes mean for your salary structure in FY 2026-27.

What Is Gratuity and Who Pays It?

Gratuity has long been governed by the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, and is now also covered by the Code on Social Security, 2020, one of the four new Labour Codes. It becomes payable when your employment ends through retirement, resignation, death or disablement, provided you meet the eligibility conditions. The employer bears the full cost; nothing is deducted from your salary for it.

The New Labour Codes in Brief

The government consolidated 29 central labour laws into four codes — on Wages, Social Security, Industrial Relations, and Occupational Safety — effective 21 November 2025. Three changes matter most for gratuity and take-home pay:

  • A broader definition of wage: Wage now means Basic + Dearness Allowance (DA) + Retaining Allowance, and it must make up at least 50% of your total CTC. Put simply, allowances cannot exceed 50% of total remuneration.
  • One-year eligibility for fixed-term employees: Fixed-term and contract staff now qualify for gratuity after one year of continuous service, instead of five.
  • Faster final settlement: Employers must complete the full and final settlement of wages within two days of an employee's resignation, dismissal or removal.

The codes also allow flexibility in how working time is arranged. For example, a company can offer a four-day week, provided weekly working hours remain capped at 48 (such as four 12-hour days).

Why the 50% wage rule changes your payslip

Many employers kept basic pay low and loaded CTC with allowances. Under the 50% rule, a larger share of CTC must be treated as wage. Because Provident Fund contributions are calculated on basic pay, a higher wage base means higher PF deductions: your retirement savings rise, but monthly take-home pay can fall slightly. Gratuity is calculated on the same wage figure, so it rises too — often substantially over a long career.

Eligibility: Who Gets Gratuity?

  • Permanent employees: 5 years of continuous service. Service of 4 years and 240 days is generally treated as meeting the condition.
  • Fixed-term and contract employees: 1 year of continuous service under the Labour Codes.
  • Death or disablement: The minimum service condition does not apply; gratuity is paid to the employee, or to the nominee or family.

A permanent employee who resigns before completing 4 years and 240 days is generally not entitled to gratuity.

The Gratuity Formula, Step by Step

For employees covered by the Act, the formula is:

Gratuity = (15 ÷ 26) × Last Drawn Monthly Wage × Completed Years of Service

  • 15 represents 15 days of wage for every completed year of service.
  • 26 is the number of working days assumed in a month.
  • Last drawn wage is Basic + DA + Retaining Allowance. HRA, bonuses and overtime are excluded.
  • Completed years are rounded up if the final year has 6 months or more of service — so 12 years 8 months counts as 13 years, while 12 years 4 months counts as 12.

There is no upper limit on years of service — the longer you work, the more you receive.

Worked Examples

Example 1: Long-serving permanent employee

Last drawn Basic + DA = ₹80,000 a month; service = 12 years 8 months, rounded up to 13 years.

  • 15 ÷ 26 × ₹80,000 = ₹46,153.85 (15 days' wage)
  • ₹46,153.85 × 13 = ₹6,00,000

The full amount is well within the ₹25 lakh tax-free limit.

Example 2: Fixed-term employee under the one-year rule

Monthly wage = ₹60,000; service = 2 years 3 months. The final 3 months are less than 6, so completed years = 2.

  • 15 ÷ 26 × ₹60,000 = ₹34,615.38
  • ₹34,615.38 × 2 = ₹69,231

Before the Labour Codes, this worker would have received nothing, because they had not completed five years.

Example 3: The effect of the 50% wage rule

Take an employee with a CTC of ₹1,00,000 a month whose Basic + DA was only ₹35,000, with the rest in allowances. Under the new rule, wage must be at least 50% of CTC, so the gratuity wage rises to at least ₹50,000. Over 10 years of service:

  • Old wage base: 15 ÷ 26 × ₹35,000 × 10 = ₹2,01,923
  • New wage base: 15 ÷ 26 × ₹50,000 × 10 = ₹2,88,462

That is about ₹86,538 more, purely from the change in how wage is defined.

Tax on Gratuity

For private sector employees, the tax-free gratuity limit under Section 10(10) was raised from ₹20 lakh to ₹25 lakh in Budget 2025, aligning it with the Central Government limit. Gratuity up to ₹25 lakh is exempt; anything above that is added to your income and taxed at your slab rate.

Payment Timeline, Forfeiture and Disputes

  • Payment deadline: The employer must pay gratuity within 30 days of it becoming due. A delay attracts simple interest of 10% a year.
  • Forfeiture: Gratuity can be forfeited only if you are terminated for moral turpitude or for wilful damage to the employer's property. In every other case, the full amount must be paid.
  • Nomination: File a nomination with your employer and update it after major life events such as marriage or the birth of a child, so the payout reaches the right person without delay.
  • If your employer refuses or delays: Approach the Controlling Authority under the Payment of Gratuity Act within 90 days. Keep your appointment letter, payslips and proof of service handy.

What to Do Now

  • Check your salary structure: Compare your Basic + DA + Retaining Allowance with your total CTC. If it is below 50%, ask HR how the company is applying the new wage definition to your gratuity and PF.
  • Fixed-term staff: Note your joining date and confirm your contract counts as continuous service, so you can claim gratuity once you cross one year.
  • Keep records: Save your appointment letter, revision letters and payslips. They are the evidence you will need if the amount is disputed.
  • Plan around the tax limit: If your expected gratuity is close to ₹25 lakh, factor the taxable excess into your retirement-year tax planning.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the gratuity calculation formula?
Gratuity = (15 ÷ 26) × last drawn monthly wage × completed years of service. Wage means Basic + DA + Retaining Allowance, and a final year of 6 months or more is rounded up to a full year.
What is the current tax-free gratuity limit?
₹25 lakh for private sector employees under Section 10(10), raised from ₹20 lakh in Budget 2025 to match the Central Government limit. Any amount above ₹25 lakh is taxed at your slab rate.
How long must I work to be eligible for gratuity?
Permanent employees need 5 years of continuous service, with 4 years and 240 days generally treated as meeting the condition. Fixed-term and contract employees qualify after 1 year under the new Labour Codes.
How do the new Labour Codes increase gratuity?
From 21 November 2025, wage (Basic + DA + Retaining Allowance) must be at least 50% of CTC. Because gratuity is calculated on wage, employees whose basic pay was earlier kept low will see higher gratuity, and higher PF contributions.
Can an employer forfeit my gratuity?
Only if you are terminated for moral turpitude or wilful damage to the employer's property. In every other case, the full amount must be paid.
What happens if my employer delays gratuity payment?
Gratuity must be paid within 30 days of becoming due, and a delay attracts 10% a year simple interest. If the employer refuses or delays, you can approach the Controlling Authority under the Payment of Gratuity Act within 90 days.
