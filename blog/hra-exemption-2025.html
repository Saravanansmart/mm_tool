HRA Exemption Guide for FY 2026-27: Formula, Metro Rules, Worked Examples and Documents

For salaried employees who live in rented homes, House Rent Allowance (HRA) is often the single largest tax exemption available — but only if you understand how it is calculated and claim it under the right tax regime. This guide explains who can claim HRA in FY 2026-27 (AY 2027-28), the "least of three" formula, how metro status changes the result, three worked examples, the paperwork your employer will ask for, and how to use our HRA Exemption Calculator to get your exact figure.

What HRA is and who can claim the exemption

HRA is an allowance your employer pays to help cover rent. Part of it can be exempt from tax under the HRA provision — Section 10(13A) in the old Income Tax Act, 1961, the reference most payroll teams still use. The new Income-tax Act 2025, which applies from FY 2026-27, reorganises and renumbers the law but retains the existing exemptions and deductions. To claim the exemption you must:

  • Be a salaried employee who receives HRA as part of your CTC.
  • Actually pay rent and live in rented accommodation, not in a house you own.
  • Opt for the Old Tax Regime. HRA exemption is not available under the New Regime (Section 115BAC), which is the default.
  • Submit rent receipts to your employer, plus your landlord's PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh.

The HRA exemption formula: least of three

The exempt amount is the lowest of these three figures, worked out on a monthly basis:

  1. Actual HRA received from your employer.
  2. Rent paid minus 10% of salary, where salary means Basic + Dearness Allowance (DA).
  3. 50% of Basic + DA if you live in a metro city, or 40% elsewhere.

Any HRA above the exempt amount is added to your taxable salary. Because only the smallest of the three counts, raising one input — say, paying more rent — often changes nothing if another limit is already lower. Since the rule is applied month by month, a change of rent or city during the year is best calculated separately for each period.

Metro vs non-metro cities

For HRA purposes, only four cities are metros: Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai. Every other city — including Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune — falls under the 40% limit. On the same salary and rent, that difference alone can be worth tens of thousands of rupees a year.

Worked examples

Example 1: metro city (Chennai)

Monthly Basic + DA ₹50,000, HRA received ₹20,000, rent paid ₹25,000.

  • Actual HRA: ₹20,000
  • Rent − 10% of salary: ₹25,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹20,000
  • 50% of Basic + DA: ₹25,000
  • Exempt HRA: ₹20,000 a month, or ₹2,40,000 a year. Taxable HRA: nil.

Example 2: non-metro city (Bengaluru), lower rent

Monthly Basic + DA ₹50,000, HRA received ₹20,000, rent paid ₹15,000.

  • Actual HRA: ₹20,000
  • Rent − 10% of salary: ₹15,000 − ₹5,000 = ₹10,000
  • 40% of Basic + DA: ₹20,000
  • Exempt HRA: ₹10,000 a month (₹1,20,000 a year). The remaining ₹10,000 a month is taxable.

Example 3: Bengaluru, rent raised to ₹25,000

With the same salary and HRA, rent of ₹25,000 gives rent − 10% = ₹20,000, while 40% of Basic + DA is ₹20,000 and actual HRA is ₹20,000. The exemption rises to ₹20,000 a month. Any rent above ₹25,000 would not increase it further, because the other two limits cap it at ₹20,000.

Example 4: rent changes mid-year

Suppose the Bengaluru employee from Examples 2 and 3 pays ₹15,000 a month from April to September and then moves to a ₹25,000 flat from October to March. Working month by month, the exemption is ₹10,000 × 6 = ₹60,000 for the first half and ₹20,000 × 6 = ₹1,20,000 for the second, giving ₹1,80,000 for the year. Averaging the rent over twelve months would give a different — and incorrect — answer.

What the exemption is worth in tax

Taking the ₹2,40,000 annual exemption from Example 1: at the 30% slab plus 4% cess, the saving is ₹2,40,000 × 31.2% = ₹74,880. At the 20% slab plus cess, it is ₹2,40,000 × 20.8% = ₹49,920.

HRA and your choice of tax regime

Since HRA is available only under the Old Regime, the exemption is often what decides which regime is cheaper. As our Income Tax Guide FY 2026-27 shows, on a ₹15 lakh salary the New Regime tax is ₹97,500, and the Old Regime only wins once deductions beyond the standard deduction exceed roughly ₹5.44 lakh. HRA alone rarely gets you there, but HRA combined with full Section 80C (₹1.5 lakh), NPS under 80CCD(1B) (₹50,000), 80D and home loan interest can. Always compare both regimes before you declare your choice to your employer.

Documents and good practice

  • Rent receipts for the full period you claim.
  • Landlord's PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh. Employers routinely reject HRA claims at the payroll stage when it is missing, so collect it before the investment-proof deadline.
  • A rent agreement showing the rent, address and tenancy period.
  • Pay rent by bank transfer. Cash payments may not stand up to scrutiny.
  • Declare to your employer early in the year so the right TDS is deducted from your salary.

Special situations

Paying rent to your parents

You can claim HRA on rent paid to your parents, provided you genuinely pay it and they declare it as their income. Keep a rent agreement and pay through bank transfer.

Owning a house in another city

If you own a house in one city but rent in the city where you work, you can claim HRA exemption and home loan benefits at the same time.

Self-employed, or no HRA from your employer

HRA exemption applies only to salaried employees who receive HRA. If you are self-employed, or your employer does not pay HRA, you may instead claim a deduction for rent under Section 80GG — capped at ₹5,000 a month (₹60,000 a year) and subject to stricter conditions.

Restructuring your salary

If your employer allows CTC restructuring, shifting more of your package into HRA can raise the exemption — but only up to the three-part limit. Beyond that, extra HRA is simply taxable salary, so the gain is capped by your actual rent and basic pay.

Common HRA mistakes to avoid

  • Using gross salary instead of Basic + DA. Both the 10% and the 50%/40% limits are based on Basic + DA only, not on your full CTC.
  • Assuming every big city is a metro. Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune use the 40% limit, however high their rents.
  • Claiming HRA after choosing the New Regime. The exemption simply does not exist there, so the choice of regime comes first.
  • Missing the landlord's PAN. Without it, rent above ₹1 lakh a year is likely to be rejected by payroll.
  • Paying more rent to "max out" HRA. Once rent minus 10% of salary exceeds the other two limits, extra rent brings no further exemption.
  • Claiming rent you don't pay. Rent to family members is allowed only if it is genuinely paid and declared as their income.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is HRA exemption calculated?
It is the lowest of three monthly amounts: the actual HRA received, rent paid minus 10% of Basic + DA, and 50% of Basic + DA in a metro city or 40% elsewhere. Any HRA above that figure is taxable.
Which cities count as metros for HRA?
Only Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata and Chennai qualify for the 50% limit. All other cities, including Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Pune, use the 40% limit.
Can I claim HRA under the New Tax Regime?
No. HRA exemption is available only under the Old Tax Regime. If you pay significant rent, compare both regimes, because HRA is often what makes the Old Regime cheaper.
What documents do I need to claim HRA?
Rent receipts for the period claimed, a rent agreement, and your landlord's PAN if annual rent exceeds ₹1 lakh. Paying rent by bank transfer makes the claim easier to support.
Can I claim HRA if I pay rent to my parents?
Yes, provided you genuinely pay the rent and your parents declare it as their income. Keep a rent agreement and pay through bank transfer.
What if I am self-employed or my employer does not pay HRA?
You cannot claim HRA exemption, but you may be able to claim a rent deduction under Section 80GG, capped at ₹5,000 a month (₹60,000 a year) and subject to stricter conditions.
