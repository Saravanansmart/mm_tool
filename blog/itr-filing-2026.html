ITR Filing Guide for AY 2026-27: Deadlines, Steps and Regime Choice

Your income tax return (ITR) for the financial year 2025-26 is filed in assessment year (AY) 2026-27. This guide covers the deadlines, what to do if you've missed one, a step-by-step filing checklist, and a worked example of choosing between the old and new tax regimes. You can check every number with our Income Tax Calculator before you submit.

Key dates for AY 2026-27

  • 31 July 2026: due date for individuals, HUFs and other taxpayers who don't need an audit.
  • 31 October 2026: due date for audit cases.
  • After the due date: you can still file a belated return, but a late fee applies under Section 234F: ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if your income is below ₹5 lakh. Check the current last date for belated returns on the income tax e-filing portal.
  • Updated return (ITR-U): if you later find income you left out, you can file an updated return within 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year, a window extended by the Budget 2025 amendment. Extra tax applies.

If you filed on time but made a mistake, correct it promptly. An accurate return now is cheaper than answering a mismatch notice later.

Step-by-step filing checklist

  1. Collect your documents: Form 16 from your employer, interest certificates from banks, capital gains statements from your broker or mutual fund registrar, and rent receipts and investment proofs if you'll use the old regime.
  2. Download and check your AIS and Form 26AS: the Annual Information Statement pre-fills salary, TDS, interest, dividends and mutual fund transactions. Compare it line by line with your own records. Unreported interest is the most common mismatch.
  3. Pick the right ITR form: it depends on your income sources (salary, house property, capital gains, business). The portal suggests a form based on your answers. If you have capital gains or business income, make sure the form you choose has the schedules for them.
  4. Choose your tax regime: compare both regimes (see below) before you submit.
  5. Report capital gains correctly: listed equity and equity mutual funds held over 12 months are taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh. Short-term gains on them are taxed at 20%. For property sold after 23 July 2024, you can choose 20% with indexation or 12.5% without, whichever gives less tax. The Capital Gains Calculator works this out.
  6. Pay any balance tax before filing, and pre-validate your bank account so any refund can be credited.
  7. E-verify within 30 days: Aadhaar OTP is the quickest method. A return that isn't verified within 30 days is treated as invalid, as if it was never filed.

Old vs new regime: which should you pick?

The new regime is the default. Its FY 2025-26 slabs are 0% up to ₹4 lakh, 5% from ₹4–8 lakh, 10% from ₹8–12 lakh, 15% from ₹12–16 lakh, 20% from ₹16–20 lakh, 25% from ₹20–24 lakh and 30% above ₹24 lakh. It offers a ₹75,000 standard deduction and a Section 87A rebate that makes taxable income up to ₹12 lakh tax-free (₹12.75 lakh of salary after the standard deduction). Most other deductions aren't allowed. The main exception is employer NPS contributions under Section 80CCD(2).

The old regime has higher rates (5% from ₹2.5–5 lakh, 20% from ₹5–10 lakh and 30% above ₹10 lakh for those below 60) and a ₹50,000 standard deduction. In return, it allows deductions such as Section 80C (₹1.5 lakh), Section 80CCD(1B) (₹50,000), HRA, Section 80D and home loan interest under Section 24(b) (up to ₹2 lakh).

Worked example: ₹15 lakh salary

New regime: taxable income = ₹15,00,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹14,25,000.

  • ₹4–8 lakh at 5% = ₹20,000
  • ₹8–12 lakh at 10% = ₹40,000
  • ₹12–14.25 lakh at 15% = ₹33,750
  • Total ₹93,750 + 4% cess (₹3,750) = ₹97,500

Old regime with ₹2.5 lakh of deductions (₹50,000 standard deduction + ₹1.5 lakh under 80C + ₹50,000 under 80CCD(1B)): taxable income = ₹12,50,000.

  • ₹2.5–5 lakh at 5% = ₹12,500
  • ₹5–10 lakh at 20% = ₹1,00,000
  • ₹10–12.5 lakh at 30% = ₹75,000
  • Total ₹1,87,500 + 4% cess (₹7,500) = ₹1,95,000

Even with another ₹2 lakh of home loan interest under Section 24(b), the old regime's taxable income falls only to ₹10,50,000 and the tax to ₹1,32,600. That's still more than the new regime's ₹97,500. For most salaried people at this income, the new regime wins unless they have very large deductions. Your figures may differ, so run both.

Switching regimes

Salaried taxpayers without business income can switch regimes every year, directly in the ITR. Taxpayers with business or professional income need to file Form 10-IEA to opt out of the new regime, and can switch back only once in a lifetime.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Leaving out savings or FD interest: it's taxable at your slab rate and already shows in your AIS.
  • Ignoring income from a previous employer: if you changed jobs, add up the salary and TDS from every Form 16.
  • Missing capital gains: redemptions and switches between mutual fund schemes count as sales.
  • Claiming old-regime deductions under the new regime: 80C, HRA and 80D aren't available there.
  • Forgetting to e-verify: without verification, the return is invalid.
  • Filing at the last minute: aim to file well before the due date. That leaves time to fix AIS mismatches and avoids peak portal load.

Frequently Asked Questions

What was the ITR filing deadline for FY 2025-26?
31 July 2026 for individuals, HUFs and non-audit cases, and 31 October 2026 for audit cases (AY 2026-27).
Can I still file my ITR after the due date?
Yes, you can file a belated return, but a late fee under Section 234F applies: ₹5,000, or ₹1,000 if your income is below ₹5 lakh. Check the current last date for belated returns on the e-filing portal. Income you missed can also be reported later through an updated return within 48 months from the end of the assessment year.
Is e-verification mandatory after filing?
Yes. You must e-verify your return within 30 days of filing, for example with an Aadhaar OTP. An unverified return is treated as invalid.
Which tax regime is better for a ₹15 lakh salary?
In most cases the new regime. On a ₹15 lakh salary, tax under the new regime is about ₹97,500 including cess, compared with about ₹1,95,000 under the old regime with ₹2.5 lakh of deductions. Run both regimes in the calculator with your own deductions.
Can salaried employees switch tax regimes when filing?
Yes. Salaried taxpayers without business income can switch regimes every year directly in the ITR. Those with business or professional income need Form 10-IEA and can switch back only once in a lifetime.
Why should I check my AIS before filing?
The Annual Information Statement shows the salary, TDS, interest, dividends and mutual fund transactions reported to the tax department. Matching your return to it avoids mismatch notices and refund delays.
