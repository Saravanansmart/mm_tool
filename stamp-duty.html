What Is Stamp Duty in India?

Stamp duty is a state-imposed tax levied on the legal recognition of property transactions. It is paid at the time of registering the sale deed with the sub-registrar's office and is calculated as a percentage of the higher of the agreement value or the circle rate (ready-reckoner value) of the property.

Because stamp duty is a state subject under the Constitution of India, rates vary widely — from 4% in Telangana to 8% in Kerala. Most states also charge a separate registration fee (typically 1%) to record the transaction. Together, stamp duty + registration can add 5% to 11% to the effective cost of buying a home.

How Is Stamp Duty Calculated?

The basic formula is:

Stamp Duty = Property Value × State Rate (%)
Registration Fee = Property Value × Registration Rate (%)
Total Cost = Property Value + Stamp Duty + Registration

The applicable rate depends on four factors:

  • State jurisdiction — each state legislature sets its own duty schedule.
  • Owner gender — most states give 1-2% concession for sole female registration to promote women's property ownership.
  • Property type — residential is standard, commercial usually adds 0.5%, agricultural land often qualifies for partial exemption.
  • Urban vs rural — some states like Haryana have higher urban rates.

Example: A ₹1 crore residential flat in Mumbai registered in a woman's name attracts 5% stamp duty (₹5,00,000) plus 1% registration (₹1,00,000) = ₹6,00,000 total — a saving of ₹1,00,000 compared to male-only registration at 6%.

Tips to Reduce Your Stamp Duty & Registration Cost

  • Register in a woman's name — savings of ₹75,000 to ₹2 lakh on a ₹1 crore flat depending on the state.
  • Time your purchase around state stamp-duty rebates — Maharashtra, Karnataka and Delhi have historically offered 1-2% concessions during COVID and pre-Diwali windows.
  • Claim Section 80C deduction — up to ₹1.5 lakh of stamp duty + registration is tax-deductible in the year of payment under the old tax regime.
  • Verify circle rate before agreement — if agreement value is below circle rate, duty is levied on circle rate. Adjust deal accordingly.
  • Consider e-stamping — most states allow online payment via SHCIL, eliminating cash-handling risk and physical stamp papers.
  • Bundle registration cost into your home loan — many banks now finance stamp duty as part of the loan, spreading the cost across your EMIs.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is stamp duty in India?
A state government tax on property transactions, calculated as a percentage of the property value. Rates range from 4% (Telangana) to 8% (Kerala) plus registration.
Do women pay less stamp duty?
Yes, in most states. Maharashtra saves 1%, Delhi 2%, UP 1%, Rajasthan 1%. Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Kerala, Gujarat currently have uniform rates.
What is the difference between stamp duty and registration?
Stamp duty (4-8%) validates the sale deed. Registration (1-4%) records the transaction in the sub-registrar's office. Both are mandatory.
Which state has the cheapest stamp duty?
Telangana at 4% base rate. However, Telangana adds 0.5% registration and 1.5% transfer duty, making the effective rate around 6% — still competitive vs Kerala's 10%+.
Is stamp duty paid on booking or possession?
On the registered sale deed, which is usually executed at possession for under-construction property. Booking amount alone does not attract stamp duty.
Can I claim stamp duty in income tax?
Yes, up to ₹1.5 lakh under Section 80C in the year of payment — but only if you opt for the old tax regime. Not available in the new regime.
