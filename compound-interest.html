What Is Compound Interest?

Compound interest is interest calculated on the initial principal plus all previously accumulated interest. Unlike simple interest, which only ever earns on the principal, compound interest creates a snowball effect — your money earns money, and that money earns even more.

This is why long-horizon investments (PPF, mutual funds, fixed deposits) deliver outsized returns. The more frequently interest compounds (monthly vs yearly), the larger your final corpus.

The Compound Interest Formula

A = P × (1 + r/n)n×t

  • A = Final amount (principal + interest)
  • P = Principal amount invested or borrowed
  • r = Annual interest rate (decimal, e.g. 5% = 0.05)
  • n = Number of times interest compounds per year (1, 2, 4, or 12)
  • t = Time in years

Total interest earned: I = A − P.

Why Compounding Frequency Matters

On ₹1,00,000 invested at 8% for 10 years, here's how the final amount changes by compounding frequency:

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a Compound Interest Calculator?
An online tool that estimates total amount and interest earned on an investment or loan, based on principal, annual rate, duration, and compounding frequency.
How is compound interest calculated?
Using A = P × (1 + r/n)n×t, where A is the total amount, P is principal, r is annual rate, n is compounding periods per year, and t is time in years.
What compounding frequencies are available?
The calculator supports monthly, quarterly, half-yearly, and yearly compounding to match different deposit and loan structures.
Is compound interest taxable in FY 2025-26?
Yes — FD and bond interest is taxed under your slab. TDS applies if annual interest exceeds ₹40,000 (₹50,000 for senior citizens). Use our Income Tax Calculator for impact.
How is this different from a SIP calculator?
This tool handles a one-time deposit growing over time. A SIP Calculator handles recurring monthly contributions with monthly compounding.
Can I use this for loan interest?
Yes — input the loan principal and the bank's compounding frequency to see the total payable. For monthly EMIs, use our Home Loan EMI Calculator instead.
